# Episode 1: The River Goddess and the Brave Prince

*Snuggle in close, little one. Tonight we begin a very old story from India, called the Mahabharata.*

Long, long ago, in a kingdom called Hastinapura, there lived a kind king named **Shantanu**. He loved to walk beside the great river Ganga, where the water went **swish, swish, swish**, and sparkled like tiny stars.

One evening, as the sky turned orange and pink, the king saw a beautiful lady standing by the water. Her eyes were calm and blue, like the river itself.

His heart went **thump, thump**. "Who are you?" he asked softly. "Will you be my queen?"

The lady was **Ganga**, the goddess of the river. She smiled and said, "I will. But you must promise me one thing. Never ask me *why* I do anything, and never stop me. If you do, I must go away."

The king put his hand on his heart. "I promise."

*(pause)*

Soon a tiny baby was born. But Ganga gently carried the baby to the river and took him back to the heavens. The king felt sad and puzzled, but he kept his promise. **Shhh.** He said nothing.

It happened again… and again… seven times. The king's heart felt heavy, but he stayed quiet.

Then an eighth baby was born, a little boy with bright, shining eyes. When Ganga walked toward the river, the king could not hold it in.

"**Stop!** Please let this one stay!"

Ganga turned and looked at him kindly. "Now I can tell you my secret. These babies were the eight **Vasus**, shining star-spirits from the sky. They had to be born on Earth for a little while, and they asked me to send them home quickly. So I did."

She held the baby close. "But this one will live a long, great life on Earth. You asked me why, so now I must go. But I will take our son, and I will bring him back when he is grown."

And Ganga melted into the river, like morning mist.

*(long pause)*

The years went by, and the king was lonely.

Meanwhile, the little boy, named **Devavrata**, grew up with his mother. Wise sages taught him to be good and kind and to read the holy books. A mighty warrior taught him to use a bow and arrow. Day after day he listened, practised, and learned, while his mother Ganga watched proudly.

*(pause)*

One day, King Shantanu was walking by the river. But something was strange. The river was **quiet**! The water was low and still.

Then he saw a tall young man on the bank with a great bow. **Zing! Zing! Zing!** He shot so many arrows, so fast, that they made a wall, and the wall held back the whole mighty river!

The king's eyes went wide. "Who is this amazing boy?" he whispered.

*(pause)*

Just then the water shimmered, and up rose Ganga, holding the young man's hand.

"King Shantanu," she said, smiling, "this is our son, **Devavrata**. He has learned everything the great teachers could teach. I promised to bring him back, and here he is."

The king opened his arms wide and gave his son the biggest, tightest **hug**. All those lonely years melted away. His heart was full, full, full of joy.

*(pause)*

**Tonight's lesson:** Good things are worth waiting for. Shantanu waited with love, and Devavrata learned patiently, a little every day.

**A question for you:** What is something you are learning, little by little?

Goodnight, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙
