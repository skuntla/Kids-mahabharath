The River Goddess and the Brave Prince

Mahabharata Bedtime Stories · Episode 1

For the grown-up: read slowly. Bold words are for acting out; · · · means a gentle pause. Pictures wait until the end!

Snuggle in close, little one. Tonight, we begin a very, very old story from India, called the Mahabharata. It is a story about a river, a king, and a prince who kept the biggest promise in the world.

Long, long ago, in a kingdom called Hastinapura, there lived a kind and gentle king named Shantanu. He loved his people, and he loved to walk beside the great river Ganga, where the water went swish, swish, swish, and sparkled like a thousand tiny stars.

· · ·

One evening, the sun was sinking low, painting the sky orange and pink. And there, standing by the water, was the most beautiful lady the king had ever seen. Her eyes were calm and blue, like the deep river itself.

The king's heart went thump, thump. He stepped closer and asked, very softly, "Who are you? Will you be my queen?"

Now, this lady was no ordinary lady. She was Ganga, the goddess of the river herself.

She smiled and said, "I will be your queen, King Shantanu. But first, you must make me one promise. You must never ask me why I do anything. And you must never stop me. If you do… I will have to go away."

The king loved her so much. He put his hand on his heart and said, "I promise."

· · ·

And so Ganga became the queen, and for a while, they were very happy.

Soon, a tiny baby was born. But Queen Ganga quietly carried the baby down to the river… and took him back to the heavens. The king felt sad and puzzled. But he remembered his promise. So he pressed his lips together and said nothing. Shhh.

Another baby came. And again, Ganga took the baby back to the heavens. This happened again… and again… seven times! Each time, the king's heart felt heavy, like a stone. But each time, he kept quiet, because a promise is a promise.

· · ·

Then an eighth baby was born, a little boy with bright, shining eyes. And when Ganga walked toward the river with him, the king could not hold it in any longer.

"Stop! Please!" he cried. "Why are you doing this? Please, let this one stay!"

Ganga turned around. She looked kind.

"Now I can tell you my secret," she said gently. "These babies were not ordinary babies. They were the eight Vasus, shining star-spirits from the sky. Because of a small mistake, they had to be born on Earth. They asked me to be their mother and send them home again quickly. So I sent seven of them back up to the heavens."

She held the baby close. "But this eighth one must stay on Earth for a long time, and live a great life."

Then she said, a little sadly, "You asked me why, so now I must go. But do not worry. I will take our son with me, and I will bring him back to you when he is grown."

And holding the baby, Ganga gently melted into the river, like mist in the morning.

· · · · ·

The king was lonely. The years went by.

Meanwhile, the little boy, named Devavrata, grew up with his mother, the river goddess. A wise sage taught him about goodness and kindness. A mighty warrior named Parashurama taught him to use a bow and arrow. Devavrata grew strong, and brave, and wise.

· · ·

One day, King Shantanu was walking by the river. But something was strange. The river was… quiet. The water had become very low and still! "What has happened to my river?" he wondered.

Then he saw it. A tall young man was standing at the riverbank, with a great bow in his hands. Zing! Zing! Zing! He shot arrow after arrow, so many arrows, so fast, that they made a wall. And that wall was holding back the whole mighty river!

The king's eyes went wide. "Who is this amazing boy?" he whispered.

· · ·

Just then, the water shimmered, and out rose Ganga, holding the young man's hand.

"King Shantanu," she said, smiling, "this is our son, Devavrata. I promised I would bring him back. And here he is."

The king opened his arms wide and gave his son the biggest, tightest hug. His heart was full, full, full of joy. Devavrata became the crown prince, and the whole kingdom loved him.

· · ·

Some years later, the king met a fisherman's daughter named Satyavati, and he wished to marry her. But her father said, "Only if Satyavati's children become the kings of Hastinapura."

The king did not want to take the kingdom from his good son, so he went home, feeling sad.

Devavrata saw his father's sad face, and he wanted to help. So he went to the fisherman himself.

"I give up the throne," said Devavrata. "Satyavati's children will be kings."

"But what about your children, one day?" asked the fisherman.

Devavrata stood up tall. He raised his hand high, and in a strong, clear voice, he said, "Then I make a promise. I will never marry. I will never have children. I will spend my whole life protecting this kingdom."

· · ·

Oh, that was a very, very big promise. So big that the sky itself was amazed! Flowers came floating down like soft rain, drifting, drifting all around him. And voices from the heavens called out, "Bhishma! Bhishma!" That means the one who made a mighty promise.

From that day, Devavrata was called Bhishma. His father was so touched that he gave him a special blessing: he could choose when his own life would end.

And Bhishma kept his promise, every single day, for his whole long life.

· · ·

Tonight's lesson: When we make a promise, we keep it, even when it is hard.

A question for you: What is one promise you can make, and keep, tomorrow?

Goodnight, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙
