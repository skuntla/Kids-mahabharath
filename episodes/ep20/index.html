Ekalavya's Gift

Kids Mahabharath · Episode 20

For the grown-up: read slowly. Bold words are for acting out; · · · means a gentle pause. Pictures wait until the end!

Last time, the princes found a forest boy named Ekalavya who had made a clay statue of Guru Drona and practiced in front of it every single day. He was so good that his seven soft arrows stopped a barking dog without hurting him. And when Arjuna asked, "Who is your teacher?", Ekalavya said, "Guru Drona!"

Snuggle in, little one. Tonight's story is a quiet one, so let's listen with soft ears.

· · ·

Walking home from the forest, Arjuna was very quiet. His face looked sad. Finally he went to Guru Drona and asked softly, "Gurudev, you promised that I would be the best archer in the whole world. But Ekalavya shoots better than me. Did you teach him secretly?"

Drona put a hand on Arjuna's shoulder. "No, Arjuna. I have never taught him. Come, let us go and see him."

· · · · ·

So Drona and the princes walked back into the forest. Crunch, crunch went the dry leaves. Can you walk quietly on tiptoe? Crunch, crunch.

There was Ekalavya, practicing in front of his clay statue. Twang! Twang! When he saw his real teacher coming, his eyes shone like two little stars. He ran to Drona, bowed down, and touched his feet. Can you fold your hands like Ekalavya?

"Gurudev! You came! I am your student. Please tell me what I can do for you."

· · ·

Now, long ago, when a student finished learning, he gave his teacher a thank-you gift. It was called guru-dakshina. Some students gave cows, some gave gold, some gave flowers.

Drona remembered his promise to Arjuna. He thought for a long, long time. Then he said, "Ekalavya, if I am your teacher, then give me your guru-dakshina. Give me the thumb of your right hand."

· · · · ·

The forest went very still. Even the parrots stopped chattering.

Now, little one, an archer needs his thumb to pull the bowstring. Without it, Ekalavya could never shoot as fast as before. But Ekalavya did not cry. He did not ask, "Why?" He did not say even one "but."

He smiled at his teacher, the way you smile at someone you love. And he gave Drona his thumb, just like that, with a brave and happy heart.

· · · · ·

Drona's eyes grew wet. He placed his hand on Ekalavya's head and blessed him. "Your love for your teacher," he said, "will be remembered for thousands and thousands of years."

And it was! We are remembering it right now, aren't we?

· · ·

The princes walked home slowly. Arjuna kept his promise to practice harder than ever. And Ekalavya? He did not give up either. He learned to hold his arrows with his fingers instead, and he kept practicing, twang, twang, in his forest by the river.

· · ·

Tonight's lesson: Ekalavya gave the hardest gift with a smiling heart. Being thankful to those who teach us, and keeping our word, is a very big kind of strong.

A question for you: Do you think Drona's wish was fair? What thank-you gift would you give to your teacher?

Next time, the princes have finished their training, and the whole kingdom comes to watch: The Great Show of Skills.

Goodnight, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙
