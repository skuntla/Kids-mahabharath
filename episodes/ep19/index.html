Ekalavya of the Forest

Kids Mahabharath · Episode 19

For the grown-up: read slowly. Bold words are for acting out; · · · means a gentle pause. Pictures wait until the end!

Last time, Guru Drona put a wooden bird high in a tree and asked, "What do you see?" Everyone saw leaves and sky and clouds, but Arjuna saw only the bird's eye. Whoosh, thwip! Drona hugged him and called him his best student.

Snuggle in, little one. Tonight we walk deep into the forest to meet a new friend.

Far from the palace, where the trees grow tall and the parrots chatter, lived a boy named Ekalavya. His father was the chief of the forest people, the Nishadas. Ekalavya had a peacock feather in his hair, a bamboo bow in his hand, and one big dream. He wanted to become a great archer.

· · ·

Ekalavya had heard about the wise teacher who trained the princes of Hastinapura. So one morning he walked and walked, all the way to the training ground. He bowed low before Guru Drona, touching the ground with his forehead. Can you bow like Ekalavya?

"Gurudev," he said, "please teach me archery too."

· · · · ·

Drona looked at the forest boy kindly, but he shook his head. "I promised to teach only the princes of this kingdom," he said. "I cannot take you as my student."

Ekalavya's eyes filled with tears. But he did not shout. He did not stamp his feet. He bowed once more and walked quietly back into the forest.

· · ·

That night, he sat by the river and thought. Then he had an idea.

He scooped up soft wet clay from the riverbank. Squish, squish, pat, pat. Can you pat the clay with your hands? Pat, pat! Little by little, he shaped a tall figure with a top-knot, a beard, and a bow. It was a statue of Guru Drona! He put orange marigold flowers at its feet and folded his hands.

"You are my teacher," he whispered. "I will learn by practicing in front of you."

· · · · ·

And so he practiced. Every morning before the birds woke up. Every evening until the stars came out. Twang! Twang! Twang! Through sunny days, rainy days, and windy days. His arrows got faster, and faster, and truer, and truer.

· · ·

One day, the princes came to the forest for a hunting trip with their guru. A little hunting dog ran ahead of them, sniffing the bushes. He found Ekalavya practicing, and he barked and barked. Woof! Woof! WOOF! Can you bark like the little dog?

Ekalavya needed quiet. So, quick as lightning, he sent seven soft arrows, zip-zip-zip-zip-zip-zip-zip! They filled the dog's mouth so it could not bark, but they did not hurt him one tiny bit!

· · · · ·

The surprised dog ran back to the princes with arrows sticking out of his mouth like a bunch of flowers. The princes gasped. Who could shoot like that?

They followed the dog's paw prints and found Ekalavya standing by his clay statue.

Arjuna stepped forward. "Who are you? And who is your teacher?"

Ekalavya smiled and bowed. "I am Ekalavya, son of the forest chief. And my teacher is Guru Drona!"

Arjuna's mouth fell open. Drona was his teacher! What would happen now?

· · ·

Tonight's lesson: When a door closes, don't give up. Like Ekalavya, keep practicing with a patient heart, and you can still learn amazing things.

A question for you: If you wanted to learn something really, really badly, how would you practice?

Next time, Guru Drona meets Ekalavya and asks him for a special gift: Ekalavya's Gift.

Goodnight, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙
