The Great Promise

Kids Mahabharath · Episode 2

For the grown-up: read slowly. Bold words are for acting out; · · · means a gentle pause. Pictures wait until the end!

Last time, King Shantanu met Ganga, the river goddess, and their son Devavrata grew up learning from great teachers. Then Ganga brought Devavrata home, and his father gave him the biggest hug.

Snuggle in, little one. Let's see what happened next.

Back in the palace of Hastinapura, King Shantanu was so happy to have his son home. He called all the people together and placed a shining crown on Devavrata's head. "My son will be the crown prince," he said, "and one day he will be king!" Everyone clapped and cheered.

· · ·

Some years later, the king was walking by the river again. Suddenly, he smelled something wonderful, like a whole garden of flowers. Sniff, sniff!

There, rowing a little boat, was a kind young woman named Satyavati. She was the daughter of the Fisherman Chief, and she helped people cross the river. Row, row, row.

The king liked her very much. He went to her father, the Fisherman Chief, and said, "I would like to marry your daughter."

The Fisherman Chief stroked his grey beard. "You may," he said, "but only if Satyavati's son will become the next king."

· · ·

The king's face fell. He could not take the crown away from his good son Devavrata. So he walked home slowly, and said nothing. But every day he looked sadder and sadder.

Devavrata noticed. He sat beside his father and held his hand. "Father, why are you so sad?" But the king would not say.

So Devavrata asked the king's wise helper, and found out the whole story. "I will help my father," he decided.

· · ·

Devavrata went to the Fisherman Chief's home by the river. Satyavati stood nearby, listening.

"Great Chief," said Devavrata, "I promise I will never be king. Satyavati's son will be king."

The Fisherman Chief said, "That is very kind. But one day, your children might want the throne, and there might be a quarrel."

Devavrata stood up tall. He raised his hand high and said, "Then I make another promise. I will never marry, and I will never have children. I will spend my whole life protecting this kingdom."

· · · · ·

Oh, what a big, big promise! The heavens were amazed. Soft flowers came floating down from the sky, drifting, drifting, all around him. And voices called out, "Bhishma! Bhishma!" That means the one who made a great and mighty promise.

From that day on, Devavrata was called Bhishma.

· · ·

Satyavati came to the palace, and she married King Shantanu. When the king learned what his son had done for him, his eyes filled with happy tears. He placed his hands on Bhishma's head and gave him a special blessing.

"My son, you gave up so much for me. So you will live as long as you wish."

And Bhishma kept his promise, every single day, for his whole long life.

· · ·

Tonight's lesson: Keeping a promise, and caring for your family, makes you truly strong.

A question for you: How can you help someone in your family tomorrow?

Goodnight, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙
