The House That Was Not Safe

Kids Mahabharath · Episode 25

For the grown-up: read slowly. Bold words are for acting out; · · · means a gentle pause. Pictures wait until the end!

Last time, King Drupada's great fire prayer gave him two wonderful children: brave Dhrishtadyumna, who stepped out of the flames, and Draupadi, who rose from the altar smelling of blue lotus flowers.

Snuggle in, little one. Tonight's story has a secret in it. Listen carefully!

Back in Hastinapura, the five Pandava brothers were growing up. King Dhritarashtra made the eldest, Yudhishthira, the crown prince. That means one day he would be king.

The people were so happy! They clapped and cheered, "Yudhishthira is kind and fair! Hooray!" Can you clap and cheer like the people?

· · ·

But one person was not cheering. Duryodhana folded his arms and frowned. Hmph! "Everyone loves the Pandavas," he grumbled. "Nobody will ever make me king."

So Duryodhana went to his father. "Father, please send the Pandavas far away to the town of Varanavata. There is a big festival there. Let them go and enjoy it."

King Dhritarashtra loved his son very much, so he said yes.

· · ·

But Duryodhana had a mean secret plan. He called his helper, a sly man named Purochana.

"Hurry to Varanavata," whispered Duryodhana. "Build a beautiful house for the Pandavas. But build it with lac, wax, straw and ghee, things that catch fire very, very easily. One night, when they are asleep, set the house on fire."

Purochana rubbed his hands. "Yes, Prince!" And off he rushed.

· · · · ·

Soon the house was ready. From the outside, it looked lovely and shiny, like golden honey. But inside, it was not safe at all.

· · ·

On the day the Pandavas left, everyone came to say goodbye. Wise Uncle Vidura came close to Yudhishthira. He spoke in a secret riddle language that only the two of them knew, so no one else would understand.

He whispered, "A fire in the forest cannot hurt the little animal that hides in a hole under the ground. And whoever knows the stars will always find the way."

Yudhishthira nodded quietly. "I understand, Uncle." Can you put your finger on your lips and say shhh?

· · · · ·

When the Pandavas and their mother Kunti reached the new house, Yudhishthira walked around and took a big sniff. Sniff, sniff!

"Bhima," he whispered, "smell that? Ghee and lac. This house is made to burn!"

Bhima clenched his fists. "Let's leave right now!"

"No," said Yudhishthira calmly. "If we run, Purochana will know we found out. Let us smile and pretend we know nothing."

· · ·

A few days later, a kind man came quietly to the house. "Uncle Vidura sent me," he said softly. "I am a digger."

Every night, while Purochana slept, the digger dug a secret tunnel under the floor. Dig, dig, dig! Can you dig with your hands? The tunnel went under the walls, far out into the forest. He covered the hole with a wooden plank, so no one could see it.

· · ·

For a whole year, the Pandavas lived in that house. In the daytime they went hunting and learned every forest path. At night they kept their weapons close, and one brother always stayed awake.

· · · · ·

Tonight's lesson: Yudhishthira listened to wise Uncle Vidura and stayed calm instead of getting scared. When we listen carefully and think before we act, we can stay safe.

A question for you: If you had a secret tunnel, where would you want it to go?

Next time, Purochana decides that tonight is the night, and the Pandavas must escape: Escape Through the Tunnel.

Goodnight, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙
