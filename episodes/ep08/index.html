The Princess with the Blindfold

Kids Mahabharath · Episode 8

For the grown-up: read slowly. Bold words are for acting out; · · · means a gentle pause. Pictures wait until the end!

Last time, Sage Vyasa came to the palace, and three baby princes were born: strong Dhritarashtra, Pandu the archer, and wise Vidura. And Uncle Bhishma looked after them with lots of love.

Snuggle in, little one. Tonight, the three little princes are all grown up!

Every morning, Uncle Bhishma took them out to practise. Dhritarashtra lifted heavy rocks, huff, puff, as if they were pillows. Pandu pulled back his bow, and twang, swish! His arrow flew straight to the middle of the target. And Vidura sat under a tree, reading old palm-leaf books, and thinking, hmmm.

· · ·

Can you show me big strong arms, like Dhritarashtra? Now pull a bow, like Pandu! Now tap your head and think, like Vidura!

· · ·

Soon it was time to choose a new king. Dhritarashtra was the oldest. But a king has to see his whole kingdom: the fields, the rivers, and all the people. And Dhritarashtra could not see.

So the wise elders chose Pandu. Bhishma placed the shining crown on his head, and everyone cheered, "Long live King Pandu!"

Dhritarashtra felt a little sad inside. But Vidura sat next to his big brother and held his hand.

· · · · ·

Then Bhishma said, "Dhritarashtra is a grown man now. It is time for him to marry."

Far away, in a land of high mountains called Gandhara, there lived a good and gentle princess named Gandhari. She prayed to Lord Shiva every day. Shiva was so pleased that he blessed her: "One day, you will have a hundred children!"

· · ·

A hundred! Can you count to a hundred? That is a LOT of children!

Bhishma sent messengers to Gandhari's father, King Subala. Clip-clop, clip-clop went their horses, all the way to Gandhara.

"Will Princess Gandhari marry our Prince Dhritarashtra?" they asked.

King Subala thought, because the prince could not see. But Hastinapura was a great and good kingdom, so he said yes.

· · ·

Then someone told Gandhari, "Princess, the prince you will marry is blind."

Gandhari was quiet for a long time. Then she took a long piece of soft cloth. She folded it, and she tied it gently over her own eyes.

"If my husband cannot see the world," she said, "then I will not see it either. We will share everything, the same."

· · · · ·

Can you gently cover your eyes with your hands, like Gandhari? What can you see? Nothing, just dark. That is how Gandhari chose to live, from that day on.

· · ·

Her brother, Prince Shakuni, took her to Hastinapura in a big chariot. Rumble, rumble went the wheels. Shakuni was very clever, and he loved his sister very much.

At the palace, the flowers were blooming and the drums went dum, dum, dum! Dhritarashtra and Gandhari were married, with Bhishma, Queen Satyavati, King Pandu and Vidura all smiling beside them.

And Gandhari kept her blindfold on, all her life long.

· · ·

Tonight's lesson: Gandhari loved her husband so much that she wanted to share everything with him, even the hard things. When someone we love has a hard time, we can stay close and share it with them.

A question for you: Who do you love to share things with?

Next time, we will meet another princess, Kunti, and hear about a magic secret blessing.

Goodnight, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙
