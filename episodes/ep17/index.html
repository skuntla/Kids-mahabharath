Guru Kripa and Guru Drona

Kids Mahabharath · Episode 17

For the grown-up: read slowly. Bold words are for acting out; · · · means a gentle pause. Pictures wait until the end!

Last time, jealous Duryodhana pushed sleeping Bhima into the river, tied up with vines. But deep under the water, the kind snake people woke him up, and King Vasuki gave him eight bowls of magic drink. Bhima came home stronger than a thousand elephants!

Snuggle in, little one. Tonight, the princes go to school!

Grandfather Bhishma watched the five Pandavas and the hundred Kauravas running and wrestling all day. "These boys need a good teacher," he said. "Someone to teach them bows and arrows, maces and swords, and how to be wise."

· · ·

So Bhishma chose Guru Kripa. Long, long ago, King Shantanu, Bhishma's own father, had found two tiny babies in the forest, a boy and a girl, lying beside a bow and arrows. He felt such kindness for them that he named them Kripa and Kripi, which means kindness. He raised them in the palace.

Now Kripa was grown up and a great teacher. Every morning the princes stood in a line. Bow up! Pull the string! Twang! Can you pull a bow like the princes? Twang!

· · · · ·

One day, the boys were playing with a little wooden ball. Bounce, bounce... Oops! The ball rolled away and fell plop! down a deep, dark well.

The princes peered in. "It's too deep!" "We can't reach it!" Yudhishthira even dropped his ring trying, plink! Now the ring was down there too.

· · ·

Just then, a thin man walked up. He had dark skin, grey hair tied in a knot, and a bow on his back. His clothes were old, but his eyes were sharp and kind.

He laughed. "Princes of the great Bharata family, and you can't get a ball out of a well? Watch this."

· · · · ·

He picked a few long blades of grass. He whispered a magic prayer and threw one blade like an arrow. Zip! It stuck into the ball! Then he threw another blade, zip! It stuck into the end of the first one. Zip, zip, zip! Blade after blade, until he had a long grass chain, and he pulled the ball right out of the well!

The boys' mouths fell open. Wow! Can you make a surprised face like the princes?

· · ·

"And my ring?" asked Yudhishthira. The man shot a real arrow, whoosh! down the well, and it came back up with the ring hanging on its tip!

The princes ran to Bhishma. "Grandfather! A wonderful archer pulled our ball out of the well with grass!"

· · · · ·

Bhishma smiled. "That must be Drona!"

Drona was the son of the wise sage Bharadwaja. He had learned all his arrow-magic from the great Guru Parashurama himself. Do you remember Parashurama with his axe, Bhishma's own teacher? Drona married Kripi, Guru Kripa's sister, and they had a little boy named Ashwatthama.

But Drona was very poor. Once he had a friend, Prince Drupada, who promised to share everything with him. When Drupada became king, he forgot his promise and sent Drona away. Drona felt very sad.

· · ·

Bhishma bowed to Drona. "Will you be the teacher of my grandsons? You and your family will have a home here, always."

Drona's face lit up. "Yes, I will teach them."

So Guru Drona became the great teacher of the Pandavas and the Kauravas, and little Ashwatthama came to learn with them too. The princes bowed and touched his feet. "Welcome, Guru!"

· · · · ·

Tonight's lesson: Don't judge someone by their old clothes. A great teacher can look very ordinary. And when we make a promise to a friend, we must keep it.

A question for you: If you had a magic bow, what would you get out of a well?

Next time, Guru Drona asks the princes a special question, and Arjuna shows that he wants to learn more than anyone.

Goodnight, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙
