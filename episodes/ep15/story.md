# Episode 15: Bhima the Mighty Playmate

*Last time, King Pandu and Queen Madri went up to the stars, and Kunti promised to love all five boys as her own. The kind sages walked Kunti and the five Pandavas all the way home to Hastinapura, where grandfather Bhishma hugged them tight.*

Snuggle in, little one. Tonight is a playful story, full of running and splashing and mangoes!

The palace of Hastinapura was busy, busy, busy. Why? Because now there were five Pandava brothers, and one hundred Kaurava brothers, and their little sister Dushala. That's a lot of cousins! Can you count to five on your fingers? **One, two, three, four, five!** Now imagine one hundred!

The oldest Kaurava was **Duryodhana**, with his little gold crown. His brother **Dushasana** followed him everywhere. All the children played together in the big palace gardens.

*(pause)*

But one Pandava was stronger than everyone. Who do you think? Yes... **Bhima!** Bhima was born on the very same day as Duryodhana, but he grew big, big, BIG. Show me your strong arms, like Bhima! **Flex!**

*(long pause)*

When the cousins had a running race, Bhima zoomed ahead. **Zoom, zoom!** The Kauravas puffed and panted behind him. "Wait for us!" they cried.

*(pause)*

When they went swimming in the palace pond, Bhima scooped up five or six cousins under his big arms. Down into the water they went! **Splash!** Then up they popped, spluttering and giggling. Can you hold your breath, like a cousin under the water? **Hmmm... pop!**

*(long pause)*

One sunny day, some Kaurava boys climbed a big mango tree to pick mangoes. Bhima looked up and grinned. He held the trunk and gave it a shake. **Shake, shake, shake!**

Down fell the mangoes, **plop, plop, plop!** And down fell the cousins too, tumbling onto the soft grass, like big mangoes themselves!

*(pause)*

Bhima wasn't trying to be mean. He just didn't know how strong he was. But the cousins got bumps and grumbles. And they never, ever won a game against him.

*(long pause)*

Duryodhana watched from the palace balcony. He crossed his arms and frowned. Can you make a grumpy frown, like Duryodhana? **Hmph!**

"Bhima always wins," he grumbled to Dushasana. "Everyone says how strong he is. I don't like it one bit!" A little seed of jealousy started to grow in Duryodhana's heart.

*(long pause)*

That night, Mother Kunti sat with Bhima in the warm lamplight. She held his big hands in hers.

"Bhima, my strong son," she said softly, "your strength is a wonderful gift. But a gift like that is for helping and protecting, not for knocking people over."

Bhima looked down at his toes. "I'm sorry, Mother. I'll try to be gentle." Yudhishthira, Arjuna, Nakula and Sahadeva smiled at their big brother. They knew he had a kind heart.

*(pause)*

So the next day, Bhima carried little cousins on his shoulders instead. **Wheee!** But Duryodhana was still watching, and still frowning.

*(long pause)*

**Tonight's lesson:** Being strong is a gift, but being gentle is a superpower. And when a friend is good at something, we can cheer for them instead of feeling jealous.

**A question for you:** What is something you're really good at? How can you use it to help someone?
