Hidimbi and Little Ghatotkacha

Kids Mahabharath · Episode 28

For the grown-up: read slowly. Bold words are for acting out; · · · means a gentle pause. Pictures wait until the end!

Last time, Bhima stayed awake all night to guard his sleeping family in the forest. He wrestled the grumpy giant Hidimba and won, and Hidimba's kind sister Hidimbi asked Mother Kunti if she could marry Bhima.

Snuggle in, little one. Tonight there is a wedding in the forest, and a very surprising baby. Ready? Let's go.

· · ·

Mother Kunti thought for a moment. Then Yudhishthira spoke gently.

"Hidimbi, you were kind to us. You may marry Bhima. But Bhima must stay with his family too. You may be together all day long, from sunrise, and every evening Bhima must come back to us."

Hidimbi folded her hands and smiled. "I promise."

So, under the tall trees, Bhima and Hidimbi put flower garlands around each other's necks. The birds sang, tweet, tweet! Can you sing like a happy bird?

· · · · ·

Hidimbi still had her magic. Every morning she held Bhima's hand and, whoooosh, she flew with him up into the sky!

They floated over snowy mountains. They splashed in cool rivers. They sat in meadows full of flowers and ate sweet fruits. Spread your arms wide and fly with them. Whoosh!

And every evening, just as Hidimbi had promised, she brought Bhima back to his mother and brothers.

· · ·

After some time, Hidimbi had a baby boy.

He was a big, strong baby with a round, shiny head, smooth like a water pot, with no hair at all! Bhima laughed and patted the little round head. Pat, pat.

"We will call him Ghatotkacha," said Bhima, "because his head is as round and smooth as a pot."

· · · · ·

Now here is the surprising part. Giant babies don't grow up slowly like you and me. Little Ghatotkacha stretched, and grew... and grew... and GREW! Stretch up tall, as tall as you can!

In just one day, the baby had become a tall, strong young man with kind, shining eyes.

· · ·

Ghatotkacha walked up to Mother Kunti and the five Pandava brothers, and he bowed down and touched their feet.

"Grandmother, Father, Uncles," he said in his deep, gentle voice, "I must go now and live in the forest with my mother. But whenever you need me, just think of me, and I will come running to help you. I promise."

Kunti hugged her giant grandson. "Bless you, my brave boy," she said.

· · ·

Then Hidimbi and Ghatotkacha waved goodbye and walked away towards the far mountains. Bye-bye! Wave goodbye with them.

· · · · ·

The Pandavas walked on through the forest. They dressed like simple sages, in plain cloth, with their hair tied up, so that no one would know they were princes.

One day, an old man with a long white beard came walking towards them. It was Grandfather Vyasa, the wise sage!

"Don't be sad, my children," said Vyasa softly. "Go to the little town of Ekachakra. Live there quietly for a while. Good days will come again."

So the Pandavas and Mother Kunti set off for Ekachakra.

· · ·

Tonight's lesson: Hidimbi kept her promise every single evening, and Ghatotkacha promised to come whenever his family needed him. A promise is a gift we give to the people we love, so we always try to keep it.

A question for you: If you could fly anywhere with Hidimbi's magic, where would you go?

Next time, the Pandavas reach the little town of Ekachakra, and they hear a family crying in the night. Why are they so sad? The Town with a Big Problem.

Goodnight, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙
