Arjuna, the Best Student

Kids Mahabharath · Episode 18

For the grown-up: read slowly. Bold words are for acting out; · · · means a gentle pause. Pictures wait until the end!

Last time, a thin stranger pulled the princes' ball from a well with a chain of grass blades, and Yudhishthira's ring came back on an arrow tip. That stranger was Guru Drona! Grandfather Bhishma welcomed him, and now Drona teaches the Pandavas and the Kauravas, with little Ashwatthama learning beside them.

Snuggle in, little one. Tonight, one boy learns to see with his heart.

Every morning at the training ground, Guru Drona lined up the princes. Soft grass under their feet, bows in their hands, and a warm breeze from the river. Bow up! Feet steady! Pull… and release! Twang! Twang! Twang! Can you stand tall like an archer? Chin up!

· · ·

The boys practiced hard. Bhima's arrows flew with a whoosh of strength. Yudhishthira aimed carefully and slowly. The twins Nakula and Sahadeva learned side by side. Little Ashwatthama practiced with a smaller bow and a big smile. Even Duryodhana and his brothers took their turns, trying to outdo one another.

But one boy practiced longer than anyone. Arjuna.

· · · · ·

When the sun went down and the other princes went home for supper, Arjuna stayed. He ate his meal quietly under the trees. One night the lamps blew out — puff! — and everything went dark. Arjuna kept eating, finding each bite with his fingers. Then he stopped and smiled. "If I can find my food in the dark," he whispered, "I can find my target in the dark too."

So he practiced at night. Twang… twang… Soft arrows in the moonlight, again and again, until his arms felt strong and his heart felt calm. Can you close your eyes and pretend to shoot? Twang!

· · ·

One bright morning, Drona set a special test. He climbed a little and placed a wooden bird high on a tree branch. Its eye was a tiny painted circle. "Princes," said Drona, "lift your bows. Tell me — what do you see?"

· · · · ·

Yudhishthira looked up. "I see the tree, the sky, the leaves, and a bird."

Bhima laughed. "I see a fat bird and lots of green!"

Duryodhana said, "I see the branch, the clouds, and my cousins standing around."

One by one the princes named many things. Drona shook his head gently. "Not yet. Look again."

· · ·

Then it was Arjuna's turn. He lifted his bow. He breathed in. The world grew quiet. He looked and looked.

"Arjuna," said Drona, "what do you see?"

Arjuna's voice was quiet and sure. "I see only the bird's eye."

· · · · ·

Drona's face lit up like a lamp. "Shoot!"

Whoosh! Arjuna's arrow flew straight and true — thwip! — and struck the bird right in the eye!

The princes gasped. Wow! Can you make a wow face with your mouth open wide?

Drona ran to Arjuna and hugged him tight. "You are my best student," he said softly. "Keep this focus, my child. I will make you the greatest archer in the world."

· · ·

Tonight's lesson: When we practice with love, and look only at what matters, wonderful things can happen. Focus is a superpower.

A question for you: If you had to look at only one thing, what would you choose to see?

Next time, we meet a boy who lives in the forest and loves archery with all his heart — Ekalavya of the Forest.

Goodnight, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙
