Escape Through the Tunnel

Kids Mahabharath · Episode 26

For the grown-up: read slowly. Bold words are for acting out; · · · means a gentle pause. Pictures wait until the end!

Last time, sly Purochana built a pretty house that could catch fire easily. But wise Uncle Vidura sent a kind digger, who dug a secret tunnel under the floor.

Snuggle in, little one. Tonight is a brave night, and a dark one. Hold my hand!

· · ·

A whole year had passed in the house at Varanavata. One day, Yudhishthira noticed something. Purochana was smiling a sneaky smile and whispering to himself.

"Brothers," Yudhishthira said quietly, "Purochana is planning to light the fire very soon. Tonight, we must go."

· · ·

That evening, Mother Kunti made a big feast. She invited many guests, and everyone ate sweet rice and fruits. Yum, yum! Then the guests went home, and the house became very quiet.

Purochana lay down by the door and fell fast asleep. Snore, snore! Can you make a sleepy snore?

It was a new moon night. There was no moon in the sky at all. Everything was dark, dark, dark.

· · · · ·

Yudhishthira lifted the wooden plank from the floor. There was the secret tunnel! "Mother, Arjuna, Nakula, Sahadeva, go in first," he whispered.

Then strong Bhima took a burning torch. "Purochana wanted to burn this house with us inside," he said. "Now his own trap will catch him."

Whoosh! Bhima lit the walls of lac. The shiny house burst into big orange flames! Then Bhima jumped into the tunnel, and pulled the plank shut above him.

· · ·

Inside the tunnel it was dark and narrow. The family crawled, crawled, crawled on their hands and knees. Can you crawl like that? Behind them they could hear the fire going crackle, crackle, crackle!

At last they came out in the middle of the forest. They breathed the cool night air. Phew!

· · · · ·

But the night was long, and everyone was so tired. Mother Kunti could hardly walk. The twins were rubbing their sleepy eyes.

"Don't worry," said Bhima. "I will carry you all!"

And mighty Bhima put Mother Kunti on his shoulders. He held Nakula and Sahadeva on his hips. He took Yudhishthira and Arjuna by the hands. Then he ran through the forest so fast that the trees bent and swayed. Zoom, zoom, zoom!

· · ·

When the sky turned pink, they reached the great river Ganga. A boatman was waiting there, standing beside a little boat.

"Who sent you?" asked Yudhishthira carefully.

The boatman smiled and whispered the secret words of Vidura's riddle. "Uncle Vidura sent me," he said. "Climb in, quickly!"

Splash, splash! The boatman rowed them across the wide river, far away from Duryodhana.

· · · · ·

Back in Hastinapura, the news came: "The house at Varanavata has burned down!" Everyone thought the Pandavas were gone. Grandfather Bhishma cried. The people cried.

But Uncle Vidura sat quietly. He wiped a pretend tear, and smiled a tiny secret smile. He knew the Pandavas were safe!

· · ·

And far away, on the other side of the river, the five brothers and their mother walked into a deep, deep forest, ready for whatever came next.

· · · · ·

Tonight's lesson: The Pandavas got through the dark night because they stayed together and helped each other. When someone in our family is tired, we can carry them too, with a hug, a helping hand, or a kind word.

A question for you: If Bhima could carry five people, how many people could you carry?

Next time, in the deep forest, the Pandavas meet a giant who lives in the trees, and his sister, who has a surprise: Bhima and the Forest Giant.

Goodnight, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙
